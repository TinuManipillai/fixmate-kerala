Using the Geyser Again This Season? Water Heater Safety Checks and Common Problems

FixMate Kerala · 19 November 2026

മലയാളത്തിൽ വായിക്കുക

From late November into December and January, early mornings around North Paravur and Aluva get a little cooler, and many families switch the bathroom geyser back on after months of not using it. Children going to school early, elderly parents and people observing the Sabarimala vratham who bathe at dawn often depend on it. A water heater uses a lot of power and sits in a wet room, so it is worth checking it properly before daily use starts.

Quick tip: switch the geyser on only for the time you need hot water, and switch it off at the wall switch before you start bathing. Never leave it on all day or overnight.

1. Check before you switch it on

  • Look at the wire, plug and socket for burn marks, melting or a loose fit
  • Make sure the geyser is fixed firmly to the wall and is not tilting
  • Check for rust, water drops or wet stains below the tank and around the pipe joints
  • Open the hot water tap first so the tank fills with water before power is given
  • Ask an electrician to confirm the geyser has a proper earth connection

2. Common problems

  • Water not getting hot: the heating element or thermostat may have failed, or the indicator light shows power but the element is not working
  • MCB or RCCB trips when switched on: often a fault in the element or wiring, or water reaching an electrical part
  • Water is too hot or keeps boiling: a thermostat fault, which needs attention quickly
  • Brown or muddy water: rust or sediment inside the tank, common with well water
  • Noise or crackling while heating: scale built up on the element

3. Small habits that help

  • Keep the temperature setting moderate, not at the highest point
  • Do not cover the geyser or hang wet towels on it
  • Do not block or remove the safety valve, even if it drips a little
  • Have the tank flushed and the element descaled from time to time if your water is hard or from a well

4. When to call for help

Stop using the geyser and call an electrician if you feel a tingle or mild shock from the tap or shower, smell burning, see sparks, or if the breaker trips again and again. Call a plumber if there is a leak from the tank, inlet or outlet pipe. Old geysers with a rusted body or leaking tank are usually better replaced than repaired.

Safety note

Water and electricity together are dangerous. Always switch off the geyser at the switch and at the MCB before touching, cleaning or checking it. Do not open the cover or try to repair the element yourself. If your home does not have an RCCB (ELCB), ask an electrician to fit one, as it can cut power quickly in case of current leakage. Keep children away from the geyser switch.
